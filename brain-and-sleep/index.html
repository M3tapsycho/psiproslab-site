Brain and Sleep: what really helps

Many pills promise a better brain. Most do little. This page shows what works and what doesn't. First the basics, because they beat every pill. Ask your doctor before you take anything, especially if you take medicine, are pregnant or are ill.

Grades: A = strong proof, B = some proof, C = little proof.

The basics that work best

  1. SleepA: 7–9 hours, at the same times every day. Get daylight in the morning, and dim screens at night.
  2. MoveA: Walking, cycling or swimming helps your thinking and your mood.
  3. EatB: Lots of vegetables, fish and olive oil, like in the Mediterranean.
  4. FocusB: Do one thing at a time and take breaks.

Melatonin: for sleep timing A for jet lag, B for falling asleep

Melatonin tells your body clock "it's night." It sets the time; it doesn't knock you out.

  • Good for: jet lag, night shifts and night owls. You fall asleep a few minutes faster.
  • How: A small dose (0.5–1 mg) works as well as a big one. Take it 1–3 hours before bedtime.
  • Watch out: It doesn't cure long-term sleep problems; a sleep therapy called CBT-I does. It can make you groggy or give you headaches or vivid dreams. Rules differ by country: in some, melatonin needs a prescription. Check with your doctor or pharmacist.

Creatine: brain fuel B

Your muscles use creatine, and so does your brain.

  • Good for: a little better memory, mostly in older people, vegetarians and people short on sleep.
  • How: 3–5 g of creatine monohydrate a day.
  • Watch out: The effect is small. If you have kidney problems, ask your doctor first.

L-theanine: calm focus B

L-theanine comes from tea.

  • Good for: together with coffee, it helps you focus without the jitters.
  • Watch out: The effect is mild. A cup of green tea gives you both at once.

Omega-3: fish oil B

Your brain is partly built from these fats.

  • Good for: a small help with low mood. Little effect on memory.
  • How: Oily fish twice a week, or about 1 g of fish oil a day.
  • Watch out: If you take blood thinners, ask your doctor first.

Lion's mane: a mushroom C

  • Good for: maybe memory and mood, but it's mostly tested on animals.
  • Watch out: Quality differs a lot between brands. A few people get allergic reactions.

Peptides such as BPC-157 C

  • Proof: Almost no good tests in people.
  • Watch out: Often sold online with unknown contents, usually as injections, and many are not approved.
  • Bottom line: Not an approved medicine and banned in sport. Best avoided.
